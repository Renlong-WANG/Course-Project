3. Ambiguity Set Design

    In the surrogate formulation, failure occurs when ξ ≤ −ĝ(X). The residual cumulative distribution
function (CDF) therefore describes the marginal probability mass relevant to each failure threshold. Its
tail accuracy matters when estimating small failure probabilities. We use the Dümbgen–Wellner (DW)
construction (Dümbgen and Wellner, 2023) to control deviations from the empirical CDF simultaneously
over all thresholds, with tolerances that vary across probability levels.
                                                           2
   We first introduce a local discrepancy that accounts for the probability level. Let q and p denote
an empirical probability and a candidate probability, respectively. Following Berk and Jones (1979) and
the CDF confidence band construction of Owen (1995), we compare them using the Bernoulli Kullback–
Leibler divergence

                                        q               1−q
                     ϕ(q, p) := q log     + (1 − q) log     ,          q ∈ [0, 1],    p ∈ (0, 1),        (4)
                                        p               1−p

where logarithms are natural and a term with zero numerator is interpreted as zero. For q close to an
interior p, the approximation ϕ(q, p) ≈ (q − p)2 /[2p(1 − p)] scales the deviation by the Bernoulli variance.
Thus, the same absolute deviation receives a larger weight near the probability boundaries. The full
divergence also covers zero and full empirical counts through ϕ(0, p) = − log(1 − p) and ϕ(1, p) = − log p.
At the boundaries of the candidate probability, we set ϕ(0, 0) = ϕ(1, 1) = 0, ϕ(q, 0) = +∞ for q > 0, and
ϕ(q, 1) = +∞ for q < 1.
   To compare deviations simultaneously across probability levels, we use the additive correction of
Dümbgen and Wellner (2023). For v ∈ (0, 1), define

4. Tractable Reformulation

   Fix the training data, the test residuals, and a threshold θ ≥ 0. We first express Q DW (θ) through
finitely many linear probability constraints and derive the Lagrangian duals of the failure probability
problems. For the default support Ξ = R, an exact aggregation then yields finite linear programs. Their
dual structure gives closed-form bounds under the original input distribution P. We assume that all test
residuals belong to Ξ, as holds almost surely under the sampling conditions in Section 3.

Proof. For the upper problem, form a bipartite flow network. Source-to-row arcs have capacities wi ,
column-to-sink arcs have capacities vj , and a row-to-column arc of capacity two is present exactly when
j ≤ i. Every transport plan restricts to a flow on these permitted pairs. Conversely, any such flow can
be completed to a transport plan using the remaining row and column masses. The maximal permitted
mass therefore equals the maximum flow.
   By the max-flow min-cut theorem (Ford and Fulkerson, 1956), this value equals the minimum cut
capacity. A cut of capacity at most one exists, so a minimum cut crosses none of the capacity-two arcs.
If the largest row index on the source side is m, every column j ≤ m must also be on that side. Adding
all rows i ≤ m and removing columns j > m from the source side cannot increase the cut capacity. Thus
a minimum cut has capacity 1 − Wm + Vm . The empty row set gives m = 0, proving (34).
   For the lower problem, maximize the complementary safe mass. The permitted pairs are j > i and
the diagonal pairs with i odd. The column neighborhoods are nested suffixes. For a nonempty source-
side row set, its earliest permitted column is either one or 2k + 1 for some k = 1, . . . , K. In the latter
case, replacing the source-side sets by all rows i ≥ 2k and columns j ≥ 2k + 1 cannot increase the
cut capacity. The resulting capacity is W2k−1 + 1 − V2k . The empty row set and the suffix starting at
column one both give capacity one. The same min-cut argument shows that the maximal safe mass is
min{1, mink (W2k−1 + 1 − V2k )}. Subtracting this value from one proves (35).

Remark 1 (Known interval support). If Ξ = [a, b] is known in advance, the measure programs and
their duals retain the domains in (19)–(24). Adding a and b to the ordered partition and removing
residual cells outside [a, b] gives the corresponding finite LPs. Input thresholds below a cannot fail, and
thresholds at least b must fail. The endpoint cuts consequently replace the outer constants one and zero
in (36)–(37) by H(−a) and H(−b), respectively.
