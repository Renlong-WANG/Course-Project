3. Ambiguity Set Design

    In the surrogate formulation, failure occurs when ξ ≤ −ĝ(X). The residual cumulative distribution
function (CDF) therefore describes the marginal probability mass relevant to each failure threshold. Its
tail accuracy matters when estimating small failure probabilities. We use the Dümbgen–Wellner (DW)
                                                           2
construction (Dümbgen and Wellner, 2023) to control deviations from the empirical CDF simultaneously
over all thresholds, with tolerances that vary across probability levels.
   We first introduce a local discrepancy that accounts for the probability level. Let q and p denote
an empirical probability and a candidate probability, respectively. Following Berk and Jones (1979) and
the CDF confidence band construction of Owen (1995), we compare them using the Bernoulli Kullback–
Leibler divergence
                                        q               1−q
                     ϕ(q, p) := q log     + (1 − q) log     ,          ∀q ∈ [0, 1], p ∈ (0, 1),          (4)
                                        p               1−p
where logarithms are natural and a term with zero numerator is interpreted as zero. For q close to an
interior p, the approximation ϕ(q, p) ≈ (q − p)2 /[2p(1 − p)] scales the deviation by the Bernoulli variance.
Thus, the same absolute deviation receives a larger weight near the probability boundaries. The full
divergence also covers zero and full empirical counts through ϕ(0, p) = − log(1 − p) and ϕ(1, p) = − log p.
At the boundaries of the candidate probability, we set ϕ(0, 0) = ϕ(1, 1) = 0, ϕ(q, 0) = +∞ for q > 0, and
ϕ(q, 1) = +∞ for q < 1.

Dümbgen and Wellner (2023). For v ∈ (0, 1), define

In the DW set, θ controls the corrected likelihood discrepancy. The corresponding CDF tolerances vary
by probability level and can differ above and below the empirical CDF. For a fixed sample size, higher

construction also proves realization of every point in this image.
