3. Ambiguity Set Design

    In the surrogate formulation, failure occurs when ξ ≤ −ĝ(X). The residual cumulative distribution
function (CDF) therefore describes the marginal probability mass relevant to each failure threshold. Its
tail accuracy matters when estimating small failure probabilities. We use the Dümbgen–Wellner (DW)
construction (Dümbgen and Wellner, 2023) to control deviations from the empirical CDF simultaneously
over all thresholds, with tolerances that vary across probability levels.
                                                           2
   We first introduce a local discrepancy that accounts for the probability level. Let q and p denote
an empirical probability and a candidate probability, respectively. Following Berk and Jones (1979) and
the CDF confidence band construction of Owen (1995), we compare them using the Bernoulli Kullback–
Leibler divergence

                                        q               1−q
                     ϕ(q, p) := q log     + (1 − q) log     ,          ∀q ∈ [0, 1], p ∈ (0, 1),          (4)
                                        p               1−p

where logarithms are natural and a term with zero numerator is interpreted as zero. For q close to an
interior p, the approximation ϕ(q, p) ≈ (q − p)2 /[2p(1 − p)] scales the deviation by the Bernoulli variance.
Thus, the same absolute deviation receives a larger weight near the probability boundaries. The full
divergence also covers zero and full empirical counts through ϕ(0, p) = − log(1 − p) and ϕ(1, p) = − log p.
At the boundaries of the candidate probability, we set ϕ(0, 0) = ϕ(1, 1) = 0, ϕ(q, 0) = +∞ for q > 0, and
ϕ(q, 1) = +∞ for q < 1.

   For every fixed N te ≥ 1, the proposition covers arbitrary residual distributions, including laws with
atoms or infinite moments. With fixed method parameters, the reference law depends only on N te , so
the threshold can be calibrated offline. Dümbgen and Wellner (2023, Supplement, Sections S.6–S.7)
provide finite-sample evaluation through uniform order-statistic boundary probabilities. Choosing θ as a
conservative upper bound on the quantile in (11) preserves (12). Calibration by simulation must control
quantile estimation error to retain the stated coverage. The threshold may also depend on the test data,
provided θ ≥ θN te (δ) almost surely; the same nesting argument then preserves (12).

(Bertsimas et al., 2018). The following theorem transfers residual-distribution coverage to simultane-
ous bounds on the failure probability under the joint model. It applies to any residual ambiguity set
satisfying the stated coverage condition.

    The event Q⋆ ∈ Q gives both inequalities, so 1 − δ is the confidence level for the entire interval. In
the DW set, θ controls the corrected likelihood discrepancy. The corresponding CDF tolerances vary
by probability level and can differ above and below the empirical CDF. For a fixed sample size, higher
confidence requires a nondecreasing calibrated threshold θN te (δ).
    For a fixed empirical CDF, 0 ≤ θ1 ≤ θ2 implies Q DW (θ1 ) ⊆ Q DW (θ2 ). The lower bound is therefore
nonincreasing in θ, and the upper bound is nondecreasing. The interval reflects finite-sample uncertainty
in the residual law and uncertainty in its dependence on the input. Because the joint ambiguity set allows
all couplings with the specified marginals, a gap can persist even when the residual marginal is known
exactly.
